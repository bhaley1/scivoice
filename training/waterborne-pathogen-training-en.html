Course Overview
Master waterborne pathogen isolation using WHO-standardized laboratory protocols
1 Learning Objectives
  • Understand WHO Guidelines for water quality testing
  • Master sample collection and transport procedures
  • Prepare standardized culture media following WHO/CDC protocols
  • Execute isolation protocols for priority waterborne pathogens
  • Interpret results and maintain quality control
  • Implement biosafety measures and laboratory safety protocols
2 Target Pathogens
Focus: This course covers isolation protocols for the most significant waterborne bacterial pathogens as defined by WHO and CDC surveillance priorities.
  • Salmonella spp. - Major cause of gastroenteritis and typhoid fever
  • Vibrio cholerae - Cholera agent, critical for outbreak response
  • Escherichia coli - Indicator organism and pathogenic strains (STEC)
  • Enterococcus spp. - Indicator organism for fecal contamination
  • Additional pathogens: Campylobacter, Shigella, Yersinia, Legionella
3 Standards Hierarchy
Protocol Priority: WHO Standards → CDC/FDA Guidelines → EPA Methods

This training prioritizes World Health Organization guidelines as the primary standard, supplemented by CDC and FDA protocols where WHO guidance is not specific, and EPA methods for environmental water testing considerations.

Sample Collection and Transport
WHO-compliant procedures for water sample collection and preservation
1 Pre-Collection Planning
Critical: Proper planning prevents contamination and ensures representative sampling according to WHO Guidelines for Drinking-water Quality.
  • Review site accessibility and safety conditions
  • Prepare sterile collection containers (500mL minimum for pathogen isolation)
  • Organize cold chain transport (4°C ± 2°C)
  • Prepare chain of custody documentation
  • Coordinate laboratory notification for sample receipt
  • Plan collection timing (morning preferred to avoid diurnal variations)
2 Sterile Collection Technique
  1. Wear nitrile gloves and replace for each sample location
  2. Remove container cap without touching inner surface
  3. Hold container at base, submerge mouth downward
  4. Rotate container underwater to fill completely
  5. Leave 2-3cm headspace for thermal expansion
  6. Replace cap immediately while container underwater
  7. Label with waterproof marker: site, date, time, collector ID
  8. Record GPS coordinates and environmental conditions
WHO Recommendation: Collect from areas with flowing water when possible. Avoid stagnant pools or areas with visible contamination that may not be representative.
3 Transport and Storage
Parameter WHO Standard CDC Guideline Maximum Time
Temperature 4°C ± 2°C 1-8°C During transport
Salmonella isolation 24 hours 24 hours From collection
Vibrio isolation 6 hours preferred 6-12 hours From collection
E. coli enumeration 24 hours 24 hours From collection
Enterococcus 48 hours 48 hours From collection
Critical Control Point: Never freeze samples intended for pathogen isolation. Freezing kills vegetative bacteria and compromises recovery.

Chain of Custody Checklist

  • Sample ID matches container label
  • Collection date and time recorded
  • GPS coordinates documented
  • Temperature log maintained during transport
  • Laboratory receipt time ≤ 24 hours from collection
  • Sample integrity verified upon receipt
Media Preparation
Standardized culture media preparation protocols following WHO/CDC guidelines
1 General Media
Medium Purpose Final pH Incubation Temperature
Buffered Peptone Water (BPW) Salmonella pre-enrichment 7.0 ± 0.1 37°C ± 1°C
Rappaport-Vassiliadis Broth (RV) Salmonella selective enrichment 5.2 ± 0.1 42°C ± 1°C
Alkaline Saline Peptone Water (ASPW) Vibrio cholerae enrichment 8.5 ± 0.1 37°C ± 1°C
MacConkey Agar E. coli isolation 7.1 ± 0.2 37°C ± 1°C
Quality Control: All media must be tested with reference strains before use. Retain control batch throughout the working life of the media batch.
2 Sterilization and Quality Control
  • Autoclave at 121°C for 15 minutes for liquid media
  • Check pH after sterilization and cooling
  • Sterility test: incubate control samples 24-48h
  • Growth promotion test with ATCC reference strains
  • Label with preparation and expiration dates
  • Store at 4°C ± 2°C, protect from light
Isolation Principles
General WHO frameworks for pathogen isolation and identification
1 Enrichment Strategy
WHO Principle: Selective enrichment enables recovery of stressed or low-number pathogens from environmental samples.
  • Non-selective pre-enrichment: Resuscitation of stressed cells under optimal conditions
  • Selective enrichment: Suppression of competing flora through selective pressure
  • Differential isolation: Separation on selective and differential media
  • Presumptive identification: Rapid biochemical tests
  • Confirmation: Serological or molecular tests
2 Quality Controls
Control Type Purpose Acceptance Criteria
Positive control Verify growth of target pathogen Typical growth observed
Negative control Verify absence of contamination No growth
Selectivity control Verify inhibition of non-target flora ≥ 99% inhibition
Salmonella Protocol
WHO-standardized protocol for Salmonella detection in water samples
1 Pre-enrichment in Buffered Peptone Water
WHO Rationale: Salmonella in environmental waters are often stressed and require gentle recovery conditions before selective pressure.
  • Add 25mL water sample to 225mL sterile BPW (1:10 dilution)
  • Mix gently by inversion 3-5 times
  • Incubate at 37°C ± 1°C for 18-24 hours
  • Do not shake during incubation (prevents oxygen depletion)
  • Check for turbidity development after 18 hours
Critical Point: Heavy contamination may require 1:100 dilution in BPW to prevent overgrowth.
2 Selective Enrichment

Rappaport-Vassiliadis (RV) Broth:

  1. Transfer 1mL of BPW culture to 9mL RV broth
  2. Mix gently and incubate at 42°C ± 1°C for 18-24 hours
  3. Observe for growth (turbidity development)
  4. Positive growth appears as increased turbidity

Alternative: Tetrathionate Broth (TTB):

  1. Transfer 1mL of BPW culture to 9mL TTB
  2. Incubate at 37°C ± 1°C for 18-24 hours
  3. TTB may recover different Salmonella serotypes than RV
WHO Recommendation: Use both RV and TTB for maximum recovery, especially in surveillance programs.
3 Isolation on Selective Media
  • Streak enrichment cultures onto XLD and HE agar
  • Incubate at 37°C ± 1°C for 18-24 hours
  • Look for suspect colonies: black/red on XLD, black on HE
  • Select 2-5 typical colonies for confirmation
  • Perform biochemical tests: TSI, LIA, urea, citrate
  • Confirm by serology or PCR if available
Vibrio Protocol
WHO Vibrio cholerae detection and Vibrio isolation methods
1 Enrichment in Alkaline Saline Peptone Water
WHO Principle: The alkaline pH (8.5-8.6) selectively favors V. cholerae growth while inhibiting most other bacteria.
  • Add 1-5mL sample to 10mL ASPW (1% NaCl, pH 8.5)
  • Incubate at 37°C for 6-8 hours for primary enrichment
  • Perform subculture: 1mL into 10mL fresh ASPW
  • Incubate additional 6-8 hours
  • Observe for characteristic pellicle formation on surface
Critical Timing: Do not exceed 8 hours per enrichment to avoid overgrowth of other alkalophiles.
2 Isolation on TCBS
  • Streak ASPW enrichment onto TCBS agar
  • Incubate at 37°C for 18-24 hours
  • Look for yellow colonies (V. cholerae, V. alginolyticus) or green/blue (other Vibrio)
  • Select 5-10 suspect colonies for testing
  • Oxidase test: positive for all Vibrio
  • Test for O1/O139 antigen for pathogenic V. cholerae
V. cholerae identification: Yellow colonies on TCBS + oxidase positive + O1 or O139 seroagglutination positive.
E. coli Protocol
CDC/EPA thermotolerant E. coli detection methods
1 Membrane Filtration Method
  • Filter 100mL sample through 0.45μm membrane
  • Place membrane on mTEC agar
  • Incubate at 35°C for 2 hours (resuscitation period)
  • Transfer to 44.5°C and incubate 22-24 hours
  • Count red/magenta colonies with yellow precipitate
  • Confirm by indole test on 10 typical colonies
Calculation: Thermotolerant E. coli = (confirmed indole+/total tested) × total colony count × dilution factor
2 Colilert Method (Defined Substrate)
  • Add Colilert reagent to 100mL sample
  • Mix until completely dissolved
  • Incubate at 37°C for 18-24 hours
  • Read: yellow = total coliforms present
  • UV confirmatory test: fluorescence = E. coli present
  • For quantification: use MPN table with serial dilutions
Advantage: Results in 24 hours, simultaneous detection of total coliforms and E. coli, no media preparation required.
Enterococcus Protocol
CDC enterococcal indicator organism procedures
1 Membrane Filtration - mE Agar
  • Filter appropriate sample volume (10-100mL depending on expected quality)
  • Place membrane on mE agar (enterococcus medium)
  • Incubate at 41°C for 48 hours
  • Look for pink to dark red colonies with black/brown centers
  • Confirm by catalase test (negative for enterococci)
  • Bile-esculin test for definitive confirmation
Interference: Some Gram-positive bacteria may give false positives. Bile-esculin confirmation is essential.
2 Enterolert Method
  • Add Enterolert reagent to 100mL sample
  • Incubate at 41°C for 24 hours
  • Observe fluorescence under UV light (365nm)
  • Fluorescence = enterococci present
  • Use serial dilutions for MPN quantification
Applications: Excellent indicators for human fecal pollution in marine and freshwaters. More resistant than E. coli to environmental stresses.
Additional Pathogens
Campylobacter, Shigella, Legionella, and Yersinia protocols
1 Campylobacter spp.
Challenges: Fastidious microaerophiles, sensitive to desiccation and temperature variations.
  • Enrichment: Preston broth at 42°C, microaerophilic atmosphere (5% O2, 10% CO2)
  • Isolation: CCDA or Skirrow agar at 42°C, 48 hours
  • Identification: Gray, moist, spreading colonies; catalase and oxidase positive
  • Confirmation: Hippurate test (C. jejuni +, C. coli -)
2 Shigella spp.
  • Enrichment: GN broth or selenite F at 37°C, 18-24h
  • Isolation: XLD, HE, DCA agars at 37°C, 18-24h
  • Identification: Colorless colonies on XLD, green on HE
  • Biochemistry: Non-motile, lactose -, usually lysine decarboxylase -
  • Confirmation: Serotyping with polyvalent antisera
3 Legionella pneumophila
  • Concentration: Filter 500mL-1L through 0.2μm membrane
  • Acid treatment: HCl-KCl pH 2.2 for 4 minutes
  • Culture: BCYE-α agar at 37°C, 3-7 days, 2.5% CO2
  • Identification: Gray colonies, growth on BCYE only
  • Confirmation: Cysteine-dependence test, serology
4 Yersinia enterocolitica
  • Cold enrichment: PBS pH 7.6 at 4°C for 2-3 weeks
  • Isolation: CIN agar (cefsulodin-irgasan-novobiocin)
  • Identification: Small, red "bull's-eye" colonies
  • Tests: Urea +, motility at 25°C, sucrose fermentation
  • Pathogenicity: Autoagglutination test at 37°C
